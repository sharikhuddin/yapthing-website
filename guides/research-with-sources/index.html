How to research a question with YapThing

Use YapThing to explore a question and request supporting links, then open the original sources yourself. Source links help you check an answer; they do not guarantee that the answer is correct.

1. Define the question and date

Include the topic, place and relevant time period. Distinguish a background explanation from a request for current information.

Example prompt: “Explain the main considerations when choosing an AI writing tool for a small team. Link to original documentation, state when you checked it, and separate verified facts from assumptions.”

2. Ask for sources that support each claim

Request original documentation, published research or other direct evidence. Tell the assistant to say when it cannot retrieve a current source instead of filling the gap.

3. Open and inspect the evidence

  • Does the linked page actually support the associated statement?
  • Is the information current enough for your question?
  • Does the source apply to your location, product version or situation?
  • Are important limitations or competing explanations missing?

4. Refine the answer

Point out unsupported claims and ask for a narrower answer. If evidence is missing, keep the uncertainty visible rather than treating a confident response as proof.

What if live research is unavailable?

Research depends on provider availability, authorization and quota. If retrieval fails, request an explanation based on available context and independently check current facts. Do not treat a general answer as freshly verified research.

Can I use this for important decisions?

Use the workflow to organize questions and evidence. Verify consequential claims with authoritative materials and appropriate professional advice.
